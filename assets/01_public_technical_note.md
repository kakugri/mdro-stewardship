# Public Technical Note

Title: Rapid MDRO Diagnostic Stewardship Workflow and LIS Alert/Comment Reference for High-Complexity Clinical Laboratories

Author: Marious Akugri

Version: 1.0 public reference release

Date: 2026-08-25

## Summary

This technical note defines a public, non-confidential reference package for laboratory-facing rapid MDRO diagnostic stewardship. The package provides reusable workflow, communication, confirmation, escalation, and documentation examples for high-complexity laboratories handling MDRO-relevant results.

The package is not a treatment guideline, institutional policy, public-health reporting system, or replacement for medical, laboratory, infection prevention, antimicrobial stewardship, or regulatory oversight. It is a practical reference framework that laboratories may review and adapt under their own local protocols.

For this reviewer-ready version, "validate" means technical review, dry-run testing against synthetic scenarios, and revision based on qualified feedback. It does not mean clinical assay validation, proof of institutional adoption, or evidence that the package has been used with patient data.

## Intended Audience

This reference package is intended for:

- medical laboratory scientists and clinical laboratory technologists;
- microbiology and molecular diagnostics personnel;
- laboratory managers and quality personnel;
- laboratory directors and technical supervisors;
- infection prevention collaborators;
- antimicrobial stewardship collaborators; and
- health-system personnel involved in diagnostic communication workflows.

The package is written from the laboratory perspective. It focuses on how laboratory result information can be verified, routed, documented, and followed up in a disciplined way.

## Terms

AMR: antimicrobial resistance.

AST: antimicrobial susceptibility testing.

C. auris: Candida auris, an emerging multidrug-resistant fungal pathogen that can spread in healthcare settings.

LIS: laboratory information system.

MDRO: multidrug-resistant organism.

Rapid MDRO signal: a laboratory result or preliminary finding that suggests possible MDRO relevance, such as a rapid molecular panel result, organism-identification concern, resistance-marker detection, resistant AST phenotype, suspected or confirmed C. auris finding, or corrected/final result that changes the communication status.

## Problem Statement

Rapid diagnostic testing can shorten the time between specimen collection, organism or resistance-marker detection, and communication to clinical teams. That speed is useful only if the result is handled with a clear laboratory workflow.

In practice, rapid MDRO-related findings may involve several points of uncertainty:

- the result may be preliminary rather than final;
- culture and susceptibility testing may still be pending;
- organism identification may require confirmation;
- the result may require infection prevention or stewardship routing under local policy;
- a corrected or final result may require follow-up communication;
- staff may need consistent language for LIS comments and alerts; and
- communication documentation may vary across shifts, sections, or facilities.

This package addresses that workflow gap. It does not attempt to decide clinical treatment. Instead, it supports a consistent laboratory communication layer around rapid MDRO-related findings.

## Purpose Of The Reference Package

The purpose of this package is to provide adaptable, non-confidential examples for:

- identifying rapid MDRO-related laboratory triggers;
- checking whether results are preliminary, final, corrected, or pending confirmation;
- routing laboratory communication to the appropriate institutional teams;
- documenting notification and follow-up actions;
- using neutral LIS comment language;
- connecting rapid results with reflex or confirmatory testing logic; and
- training laboratory staff on the distinction between laboratory communication and clinical decision-making.

## Scope

This package is limited to laboratory diagnostic communication and stewardship support.

It covers:

- rapid molecular result communication;
- microbiology and organism-identification workflow considerations;
- AST status and follow-up communication;
- C. auris suspicion, identification, screening, and transfer-communication awareness;
- LIS alert/comment language examples;
- reflex and confirmatory testing considerations;
- TAT and escalation metrics; and
- staff training concepts.

It is designed for public review and adaptation. It uses synthetic examples and public guidance, not patient records or employer-controlled procedures.

## Non-Scope

This package does not:

- prescribe antimicrobial or antifungal therapy;
- recommend a specific drug, dosage, or treatment duration;
- direct patient-specific clinical decisions;
- replace clinical judgment;
- replace institutional policy;
- replace laboratory director approval;
- replace CLIA, CAP, state, local, or institutional requirements;
- establish a public-health reporting system;
- claim adoption by any employer, hospital, public-health agency, or professional body; or
- use confidential employer information or patient data.

## Design Principles

### 1. Laboratory-Scoped

The artifacts focus on what laboratory personnel can reasonably document, verify, communicate, and escalate under local policy. The package does not cross into prescribing, diagnosis ownership, infection-control orders, or treatment selection.

### 2. Non-Confidential

The examples are synthetic. They should not contain patient names, medical record numbers, accession numbers, dates of service, facility identifiers, local screenshots, internal SOP language, or employer data.

### 3. Public And Portable

The package is meant to be readable outside one institution. It uses generic workflow categories and neutral wording so other laboratories can evaluate and adapt the framework within their own governance structures.

### 4. Confirmation-Aware

Rapid molecular or organism-identification findings should be communicated with attention to result status. Preliminary findings, pending culture or AST, confirmatory testing, corrected results, and final reports may require different comments, routing, and follow-up.

### 5. Measurement-Aware

The package includes a practical metric plan so a laboratory can track whether communication steps are happening consistently. Example measures include time from verified result to documented notification, final-result follow-up completion, and staff training completion.

### 6. Locally Governed

Any implementation must be reviewed and approved by the relevant local authorities, such as the laboratory director, medical director, infection prevention, antimicrobial stewardship, quality, information technology, and compliance teams.

The public package may help organize that review, but it does not itself authorize a laboratory to change live LIS comments, notification routes, report wording, or clinical workflows.

## Public Source Basis

This package is informed by public guidance from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

Relevant source themes include:

- Diagnostic excellence and diagnostic stewardship emphasize appropriate test use, interpretation, communication, multidisciplinary teamwork, and systems that support timely diagnosis.
- Antibiotic stewardship programs promote appropriate antibiotic use and efforts to combat antimicrobial resistance.
- MDRO containment guidance addresses response to novel and targeted MDROs in healthcare facilities and public-health settings.
- C. auris guidance emphasizes healthcare transmission risk, staff education, infection-control communication, and communication of C. auris status during transfer or discharge.
- CDC laboratory guidance for C. auris describes the importance of early accurate identification, reporting, MALDI-TOF, PCR, and laboratory communication with infection prevention/public-health channels.

These sources provide background. The specific contribution of this reference package is the laboratory-facing communication workflow and accompanying artifacts.

## Artifact Roadmap

This technical note anchors a six-part package.

### Artifact 1: Public Technical Note

Defines the package, scope, source basis, boundaries, intended audience, and local adaptation model.

### Artifact 2: Rapid MDRO Result-To-Action Decision Tree

Provides a high-level workflow from rapid MDRO-related trigger to laboratory verification, communication routing, reflex/confirmatory review, and documentation.

### Artifact 3: Reflex And Confirmatory Testing Matrix

Maps common rapid MDRO-related trigger types to preliminary/final/corrected result status, suggested laboratory checks, possible reflex or confirmatory paths, communication actions, and caveats.

### Artifact 4: LIS Alert/Comment Template Bank

Provides neutral, adaptable LIS comment examples for preliminary rapid results, resistance-marker detection, organism identification pending confirmation, AST pending/finalized status, C. auris concerns, interfacility transfer communication reminders, and corrected/final result follow-up.

### Artifact 5: TAT And Escalation Metric Plan

Defines practical metrics for tracking communication consistency, result-to-notification timing, final-result follow-up, training completion, and artifact feedback incorporation.

### Artifact 6: Staff Training Checklist

Provides a checklist and synthetic case exercises for laboratory staff reviewing rapid MDRO communication workflows.

## Local Adaptation Model

Laboratories reviewing this package should adapt it through local governance. A safe adaptation process may include:

1. Laboratory director or medical director review.
2. Quality and compliance review.
3. Infection prevention and antimicrobial stewardship review where applicable.
4. LIS/informatics review for any local comment or alert changes.
5. Staff training and competency documentation.
6. Pilot review using synthetic or approved non-patient examples before any live workflow change.
7. Ongoing monitoring of communication and follow-up metrics.

This model is intentionally modular. A laboratory may adapt the LIS comment templates without using the full metric plan, or may use the training checklist to review existing local procedures.

Any local adaptation should replace the synthetic examples with locally approved training or implementation materials only through approved systems and privacy/compliance review.

## Example Use Case

The following synthetic scenario illustrates the package boundary.

```text
Scenario:
A rapid molecular blood culture panel detects an organism and a resistance marker. Culture and full AST are still pending.

Laboratory workflow supported by this package:
1. Confirm result status and internal review/QC completion.
2. Use locally approved notification route if the result meets urgent or critical communication criteria.
3. Add neutral LIS comment language that notes rapid detection and pending culture/AST status.
4. Route infection prevention or stewardship awareness according to institutional policy.
5. Track whether final AST or corrected results require follow-up communication.

Out of scope:
The laboratory artifact does not select antibiotic therapy, recommend a medication, or override institutional protocols.
```

## Review Questions For Technical Reviewers

Reviewers may evaluate this package by asking:

- Is the scope appropriately limited to laboratory communication and diagnostic stewardship support?
- Are the examples clear enough for laboratory professionals to understand?
- Do the workflows respect preliminary, final, corrected, and pending-result distinctions?
- Are the LIS comment examples neutral and non-prescriptive?
- Could the artifacts be adapted by more than one high-complexity laboratory?
- Are any claims too broad or unsupported?
- Are there additional safety boundaries that should be added?
