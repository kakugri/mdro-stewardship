# Staff Training Checklist

Title: Laboratory Staff Checklist For Rapid MDRO Result Communication Workflows

Author: Marious Akugri

Version: 1.0 public reference release

Date: 2026-08-25

## Summary

This checklist provides a synthetic training framework for laboratory staff reviewing rapid MDRO-related result communication workflows. It is intended to support consistent understanding of result status, verification, communication routing, LIS comment use, final-result follow-up, and documentation.

This checklist is not an institutional training record, competency assessment, clinical protocol, or treatment guide. Local laboratories must adapt and approve any training through their own governance processes.

## Intended Learners

This checklist may be adapted for:

- medical laboratory scientists;
- clinical laboratory technologists;
- microbiology personnel;
- molecular diagnostics personnel;
- laboratory supervisors;
- quality personnel; and
- staff who participate in result review, release, notification, documentation, or final-result follow-up.

## Training Objectives

After reviewing the workflow package, staff should be able to:

1. identify common rapid MDRO-related result triggers;
2. distinguish preliminary, final, corrected, pending, and confirmation-dependent result status;
3. recognize when culture, AST, organism identification, or confirmatory testing may still be pending;
4. apply neutral, non-prescriptive LIS comment logic;
5. follow local communication pathways for urgent, infection prevention, stewardship, or technical-review routing;
6. document notification and follow-up according to local policy;
7. reconcile rapid preliminary results with final or corrected results; and
8. understand what laboratory staff should not do when communicating rapid MDRO-related findings.

## Training Boundary

This checklist does not train staff to:

- prescribe antimicrobial or antifungal therapy;
- select a drug or treatment plan;
- determine isolation orders;
- decide transfer acceptance or denial;
- replace infection prevention or antimicrobial stewardship judgment;
- bypass local laboratory director or medical director approval;
- create unapproved LIS comments; or
- use patient data in public examples.

## What Laboratory Staff Should Do

| Action | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Confirm result status before communication. | Avoid overstatement of preliminary or pending results. |
| Confirm internal review/QC completion. | Support reliable result release. |
| Use locally approved communication routes. | Keep communication consistent and auditable. |
| Use neutral LIS comments. | Communicate result status without directing treatment. |
| Track pending culture, AST, confirmation, final, or corrected results. | Prevent rapid preliminary results from becoming orphaned. |
| Document notification and follow-up according to local policy. | Support quality review and accountability. |
| Escalate unusual, discrepant, corrected, or confirmation-dependent results. | Ensure appropriate technical review. |

## What Laboratory Staff Should Not Do

| Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|
| Do not select therapy. | Treatment decisions belong to clinical teams under institutional protocols. |
| Do not give drug-specific recommendations in LIS comments. | The lab comment should communicate laboratory status, not prescribe. |
| Do not state preliminary findings as final. | Preliminary rapid results may need culture, AST, or confirmatory follow-up. |
| Do not ignore corrected reports. | Corrected results may change prior communication status. |
| Do not use unapproved LIS comments. | Comments must be reviewed by local governance before live use. |
| Do not export patient identifiers into training examples. | Public or informal training examples must remain non-confidential. |
| Do not claim external adoption without documentation. | Public artifacts are reference materials unless adopted locally through approval. |

## Synthetic Case Exercise 1: Rapid Molecular Result With AST Pending

```text
Scenario:
Rapid molecular testing detects Organism X and Resistance Marker Y from a positive blood culture bottle. Culture and AST are pending.
```

Staff questions:

1. Is the result preliminary, final, corrected, or pending confirmation?
2. What internal review/QC step must be complete before communication?
3. Which local communication route may apply?
4. What neutral LIS comment category fits this result?
5. What follow-up should be tracked when AST becomes available?

Expected training points:

- Do not imply final AST is complete.
- Use local urgent/critical route only if criteria are met.
- Document notification if required.
- Track final culture and AST follow-up.

## Synthetic Case Exercise 2: Suspected C. auris Pending Confirmation

```text
Scenario:
A yeast identification result raises concern for Candida auris, but confirmatory identification is pending.
```

Staff questions:

1. Can this be reported as confirmed C. auris?
2. What confirmatory or technical-review route may apply?
3. Which local infection prevention route may apply?
4. What LIS comment language avoids overstatement?
5. What should be documented?

Expected training points:

- Do not report confirmation before criteria are met.
- Escalate through local technical and infection prevention procedures.
- Use neutral pending-confirmation language.
- Follow local public-health reporting rules where applicable.

## Synthetic Case Exercise 3: Corrected Result Follow-Up

```text
Scenario:
A preliminary organism identification was communicated. Later, additional testing changes the organism identification and a corrected report is issued.
```

Staff questions:

1. What changed between the initial and corrected result?
2. Does local corrected-result communication policy apply?
3. Which prior recipients or routes may require follow-up?
4. Does an LIS comment or result note need updating?
5. How should follow-up be documented?

Expected training points:

- Corrected results require careful documentation.
- Prior communication may need follow-up.
- Follow local corrected-reporting procedure.

## Synthetic Case Exercise 4: Negative Rapid Panel With Culture Pending

```text
Scenario:
A rapid panel does not detect organisms or resistance markers included on the panel, but culture workup is still pending.
```

Staff questions:

1. What does the negative rapid result cover?
2. What remains pending?
3. What comment language avoids over-reassurance?
4. What final-result follow-up is needed?

Expected training points:

- Negative rapid results may not exclude all organisms or mechanisms.
- Culture/final result workflow should continue.
- Avoid language that closes the case prematurely.

## Competency Confirmation Prompts

A local laboratory adapting this checklist may ask staff to confirm:

- I understand the difference between preliminary and final result status.
- I understand that LIS comments must not direct treatment.
- I understand that C. auris concern and confirmation require local technical and infection prevention procedures.
- I understand how to document notification according to local policy.
- I understand when final or corrected results require follow-up.
- I understand that synthetic examples must not be replaced with patient data outside approved systems.
