## Purpose

This technical review guide is for reviewers evaluating the public artifact suite titled:

```text
Rapid MDRO Diagnostic Stewardship Workflow and LIS Alert/Comment Reference for High-Complexity Clinical Laboratories
```

The review should focus on technical coherence, laboratory scope, practical usability, safety boundaries, and adaptability. Reviewers are not being asked to make legal conclusions.

This public reference release is ready for technical review. Reviewers may focus on the lane most relevant to their expertise:

- microbiology, molecular diagnostics, AMR, AST, TAT, and workflow optimization;
- public-health and diagnostic communication;
- laboratory quality, regulatory documentation, specimen/result workflow, and non-confidential boundaries.

## Fast Review Path

If time is limited, reviewers can still provide useful technical feedback by following this sequence:

1. Confirm the scope boundary in the implementation map and public technical note.
2. Review the result-status taxonomy.
3. Walk through at least three synthetic scenarios: one rapid molecular/resistance-marker case, one C. auris case, and one corrected or discrepant result case.
4. Check whether the decision tree, matrix, and LIS comment examples handle those scenarios without overstating the result or directing treatment.
5. Return either the feedback checklist, a short memo, or a signed review letter limited to the reviewer's own expertise.

## Review Scope

Please evaluate whether the artifact suite:

- is technically coherent from a clinical laboratory perspective;
- is appropriately limited to laboratory communication and diagnostic stewardship support;
- avoids prescribing or patient-specific treatment direction;
- distinguishes preliminary, final, corrected, pending, and confirmation-dependent results;
- uses result-status terminology consistently across artifacts;
- uses non-confidential and synthetic examples;
- provides practical communication and documentation logic;
- can be dry-run against synthetic scenarios before any local implementation;
- could be adapted by more than one high-complexity clinical laboratory under local governance; and
- identifies reasonable safety boundaries.

## Review Questions

### Technical Coherence

1. Does the decision tree reflect a realistic laboratory workflow for rapid MDRO-related findings?
2. Does the matrix handle preliminary, final, corrected, pending, and discrepant results clearly?
3. Are the C. auris examples appropriately cautious about confirmation status?
4. Are culture, organism identification, AST, and rapid molecular findings distinguished clearly?
5. Do the synthetic scenarios expose any missing workflow steps?

### Laboratory Scope

1. Does the suite stay within laboratory communication and diagnostic stewardship support?
2. Does any section appear to direct clinical care, medication choice, isolation orders, or transfer decisions?
3. Are local policy, laboratory director, medical director, infection prevention, stewardship, quality, and LIS governance roles respected?

### Practical Use

1. Are the LIS comment examples clear and neutral?
2. Are the comments adaptable to a local LIS environment after approval?
3. Are the metric categories reasonable for evaluating workflow consistency?
4. Are the staff training cases realistic enough for educational use?
5. Does the reviewer feedback checklist capture the most important practical review questions?

### Safety And Confidentiality

1. Does the suite avoid patient data and employer-confidential procedures?
2. Are synthetic examples clearly marked?
3. Are there any claims of adoption, implementation, or institutional approval that should be removed?
4. Are there any sections that need stronger warnings or boundaries?

### Adaptability

1. Could a high-complexity clinical laboratory adapt parts of this package under local governance?
2. Which artifact is strongest?
3. Which artifact needs the most revision?
4. What additional artifact would make the package more useful?

## Suggested Reviewer Statement Topics

If a reviewer later chooses to write a signed technical review letter, useful topics may include:

- professional background and relevance to clinical laboratory, microbiology, molecular diagnostics, infection prevention, stewardship, quality, or healthcare operations;
- materials reviewed;
- whether the suite is technically coherent;
- whether the suite is laboratory-scoped and non-prescriptive;
- whether the suite addresses a practical communication gap around rapid MDRO-related results;
- whether the artifacts are public, non-confidential, and adaptable;
- whether the synthetic scenarios and result-status taxonomy make the package easier to review or adapt;
- whether Marious's documented laboratory background appears relevant to the work;
- limitations or revisions recommended; and
- whether continued development and review of the suite would be useful.

The reviewer should avoid legal conclusions, immigration conclusions, unsupported adoption claims, and statements that the package is an approved institutional protocol.

The reviewer should also avoid saying the package has been clinically validated, implemented, or adopted unless they have direct evidence of that fact. A technical review letter can be strong without claiming adoption.
